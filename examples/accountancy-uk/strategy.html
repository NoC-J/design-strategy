Design strategy / 26 November 2026 / Small accountancy practice, United Kingdom

A named accountant who replies within a day, at a price that says what it covers.

We looked at 12 accountancy businesses across three levels (UK, Europe and global) and opened 3 of their sites. The big online players sell speed and scale. Customers complain about slow replies, mixed answers and extras on the bill. A small practice can win by promising the opposite, and keeping the promise.

Our position

Your own accountant: a named person, a reply within one working day, one clear monthly price.

What everyone does

Sells with stars and big customer numbers, and a "get a quote" button. Crunch shows 1,298+ five-star reviews and 64,000+ UK businesses.

3

What nobody does

None of the 3 sites we opened promises a reply time. Only Crunch speaks about UK rules (Making Tax Digital).

4

First move

A page that shows the full price list, what is included, and a 2-minute "Do I need Making Tax Digital?" check.

So what? From April 2026, sole traders and landlords earning over £50,000 must report through Making Tax Digital for Income Tax. The limit drops to £30,000 in April 2027 and £20,000 in April 2028. A growing number of small traders will need help and will search for plain answers.

The practice and its customer

Assumptions (please confirm): a small UK accountancy practice serving sole traders, landlords and very small limited companies, with a few staff and local or remote clients. We were given no name or town, so this report treats it as an independent practice. Market: United Kingdom.

Who buys

  • Sole traders filing Self Assessment who are new to Making Tax Digital
  • Landlords with rental income that counts towards the limit
  • Small company directors who want one person to ask

What stops them buying

  • "What will it really cost, with extras?"
  • "Will someone actually answer when I ask?"
  • "Is this person qualified and regulated?"
  • "Do I even need this new HMRC system?"

So what? Every site tells you how many people trust it. None of them tells you how fast a person will answer you.

What people charge

Entry monthly prices for a sole trader, as the firms advertise them (seen through search, not on pages we opened). VAT is treated differently by each firm, so treat this as a rough guide.

So what? Prices cluster between about £22 and £40 a month. A small practice does not win on the lowest price. It wins by explaining exactly what that price includes.

Our design direction

Follow

  • Show a review count and any regulator or body you belong to
  • Keep the phone number in the menu
  • Keep a clear quote or booking button

Break

  • No hidden prices. Show the list and what each plan includes and excludes.
  • Show real people with names and qualifications
  • Promise a reply time, then track it
  • No startup or AI language

Colour

The firms we saw use magenta and navy (Crunch), navy and mint green (Bench) and purple with yellow (Pilot). A calm deep teal on warm paper feels trustworthy and different.

Type, tone and layout

  • Headings: a calm serif (for example Source Serif). Body: a plain sans. Narrow column, generous space, thin lines.
  • Tone: plain and warm. "Here is what you pay and what you get." Explain Making Tax Digital in two sentences.
  • Photos: the real team. Add name, role and qualification under each.
