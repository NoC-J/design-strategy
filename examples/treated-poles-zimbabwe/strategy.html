Design strategy / November 2026 / Treated pole sales, Zimbabwe

Be the pole supplier who tells you what to buy, what it costs, and when it arrives.

We looked at 12 businesses across three levels (local, regional and global) and opened 10 of their websites (3 of those only as plain text). Local pole sellers say who they are. Almost none help a buyer decide. That is the gap.

At a glance

1

Our position

The practical pole specialist: clear sizes, clear price guide, clear delivery, backed by the SAZ standard mark.

2

What local sites do

Say who they are and what they treat. Show standards and customers (ZESA, REA). Show no prices, no size charts, no delivery terms.

3

What best sites do

Global sellers show price, minimum order, lead time and how deep to bury the pole, then offer a fast quote.

4

First move

Build a one-page "Pick your pole" guide with price ranges and a WhatsApp quote button. It works on a phone with a weak signal.

So what? Buyers are choosing between near-identical suppliers. The one that answers "which pole, how much, how soon?" without a phone call wins the first enquiry. No local site we read does this yet.

The business and its customer

Assumptions (please confirm): the business sells treated timber poles in Zimbabwe (as a treater or a reseller). We were not given a name, so this report treats it as a new or growing supplier. We worked this out from the local names you mentioned (Eastern Forest, Lomagundi Poles).

Who buys

  • Farmers and fencing contractors: fence poles, droppers, greenhouse and trellis poles
  • Power and telecoms contractors: transmission and telephone poles (ZESA, REA, TelOne work)
  • Mines, builders, grain stores: props, dunnage, roofing poles

What stops them buying

  • "Is it really treated, and to the right standard?"
  • "Creosote or CCA, which one do I need?"
  • "How long and how thick should the pole be?"
  • "What will it cost, and can you deliver to my farm or site?"

Our guess from the pages read; no customer interviews yet

Goal of the website: get a qualified quote request (size, quantity, place) by WhatsApp, phone or form.

Why creosote vs CCA matters here: producers are switching and short of chemicals. Allied moved a creosote plant to CCA because creosote was in short supply, and Border Timbers sought alternative chemicals. A buyer needs a plain explanation of the difference, and the supplier who gives it earns trust.

Our design direction

Follow

  • Show the SAZ mark and standard numbers (buyers expect them)
  • Name real customers once you have permission
  • Keep a clear "Request Quote" in the menu

Break

  • No company-name headlines. Lead with the buyer's question.
  • No hidden prices. Show "from" ranges, with a note that final quotes vary.
  • No long menus. Five items at most.

Colour

Competitors lean on greens, gold or black. We use warm earth tones that look like cut timber and Zimbabwean soil.

Type, tone and phone

  • Headings: a strong condensed sans such as Barlow Condensed. Body: a plain sans such as Source Sans 3. Fall back to system fonts so pages load fast on slow data.
  • Tone: short, direct, practical. "A 6 m, 100–125 mm fence pole lasts about X years in dry ground." (confirm real figures with your treater)
  • Phone first: big tap targets, a sticky WhatsApp button, pages under about 1 MB.
