Design strategy / 26 November 2026 / Independent skincare brand, United Kingdom

Show your proof before your price.

We looked at 13 skincare brands across three levels (UK, Europe and global) and opened 5 of their sites. Every site we could open led with a sale. A small brand that calmly shows what is in the bottle, who it suits and what happens if it goes wrong will stand out.

At a glance

1

Our position

The honest skincare brand: clear ingredients, clear skin-type advice, and a plain promise on delivery and returns.

2

What everyone does

All 5 sites we opened led with a Black Friday offer. Two put an email pop-up over the first screen.

3

What nobody does

Show what happens if the product does not suit you. We found no returns or guarantee message on any first screen.

4

First move

Build a calm first screen: one promise, proof badges, a 3-question skin-type picker, and a delivery and returns line.

So what? We looked during Black Friday, when every brand shouts about discounts. A new brand cannot win a discount race. It can win on trust, and trust is what buyers say goes wrong (late orders, no replies, slow refunds).

The brand and its customer

Assumptions (please confirm): a small independent UK skincare brand selling mostly through its own online shop, with no existing big-name reputation. We were given no name, so this report treats it as a new brand. Market: United Kingdom.

Who buys

  • Ingredient-curious shoppers who read labels and compare brands
  • Sensitive-skin buyers who fear a bad reaction
  • Gift buyers who want something that looks and feels special

What stops them buying

  • "Will this suit my skin?"
  • "Can I trust a brand I have not heard of?"
  • "What if it arrives late or I need to send it back?"
  • "Is the discount hiding a high normal price?"

UK brands vs. the best sites

What the clearest global site says

"Here is exactly what you pay, what shipping costs and where to start."

Where to stand: keep the UK strengths (proof badges, review counts) and add the global habit of stating delivery terms right at the top. Go one step further and state the returns promise too.

Our design direction

Follow

  • Show standards badges (vegan, cruelty-free, B Corp where true)
  • Show review counts and real review text
  • Show prices in the menu or on the cards
  • Offer help by skin type

Break

  • No sale headline as the brand's first words
  • No pop-up before the page is seen. Offer email later.
  • No hidden returns policy. Say it near the top.
  • No pink, mint, cyan or electric blue: the others own them

Colour

The brands we saw use sage and coral (Pai), mint and yellow on black (UpCircle), cyan on black (INKEY), hot pink (BYBI) and electric blue (Glossier). Warm oat, plum and terracotta are free.

Type, tone and speed

  • Headings: a soft, warm serif (for example Fraunces). Body: a clean sans. Fall back to system fonts so the first screen loads quickly on mobile data.
  • Tone: warm and plain. "Here is what is in it and why." No miracle claims.
  • Images: real skin, real hands, real bottles. Check that the main image loads fast, because two sites we opened showed blank image areas.

  • Write the delivery and returns promise you can keep
  • Get standards and testing paperwork in order
  • Photograph real skin and products

Days 31–60

  • Launch the calm first screen and the routine picker
  • Start the review request email

Days 61–90

  • Pitch editors with early reviews
  • Trial one marketplace
  • Check a normal week against Black Friday sites

Risks, open questions and limits

Open questions

  • Brand name, range, price tier and what is truly certified
  • What delivery and returns promise can the team keep?
  • Who is the first customer: gift buyer or sensitive-skin buyer?

What this report could not check

  • It was run during Black Friday. Re-check in a normal week.
  • INKEY and Glossier showed their US stores to our browser; the UK stores were not seen.
  • Typology, Benecos, Aesop and Milk Makeup were not opened
  • First screens only. Footer policies were not read.
  • Reviews: one search, no interviews
